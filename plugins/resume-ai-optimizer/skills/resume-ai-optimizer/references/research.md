# Research behind the rules

Source: Jeff Su, "Write a Killer Résumé in the AI Era: 5 New Rules!", YouTube, 2026-09-15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N1Fm4JYae1I, with the written guide at https://www.jeffsu.org/resume-ai-era-guide/. The video summarizes hiring reports and studies, including work from MIT and Oxford, covering more than 4,000 hiring managers and nearly 2 million applications. The individual studies are not linked there, so quote these numbers as "hiring research summarized by Jeff Su", not as primary sources.

## Two stages

- Companies use AI to read, rank and organize applications.
- Only 6% of hiring managers say AI makes the pass or reject decision. Humans still decide.

## Rule 1: the AI can read it

- 87% of hiring managers say their AI hiring software reads simple, text-based resumes more accurately than visual ones.
- Some systems state they cannot parse files above 2.5 MB. High-resolution images are a common cause.
- Check: open the PDF and try to select and copy the text.

## Rule 2: the fit is obvious

- Across nearly 2 million applications, untailored resumes got a 3.09% interview rate, tailored ones 5.71%, which is 84% higher.
- Resumes with the highest keyword coverage got 21% fewer interviews than those with moderate coverage.
- Keyword mapping uses the posting's terms for work the candidate has done. Keyword stuffing crams in every term regardless of evidence.

## Rule 3: AI stops at the facts

- 59% of hiring managers see AI use as a good sign. 28% reject AI-heavy resumes that show little effort.
- An MIT randomized experiment with nearly half a million job seekers: AI help with spelling, grammar and wording raised the probability of being hired by 8%.
- A separate experiment: giving applicants ChatGPT made their pitches more alike and cut evaluators' ability to screen by up to 9%.
- Method: the candidate brain-dumps result, own contribution and how for each role. AI only sharpens wording and keeps the facts.

## Rule 4: impact in numbers

- Resumes that quantified impact got 75% higher interview rates than those that listed responsibilities.
- Google's XYZ formula: accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z.
- Metrics beyond revenue: time saved, speed, scale, accuracy.

## Rule 5: AI skills are proven

- 60% of hiring managers want candidates to prove AI skills, not just list them.
- Preferred proof: 26% an interview or task, 19% work examples or outcomes, 15% certifications or courses.
- An Oxford experiment: adding role-relevant AI skills raised the chance of an interview by up to 15 percentage points.
- 86% of hiring managers value relevant work experience over formal education, so Experience goes above Education except for recent graduates.
